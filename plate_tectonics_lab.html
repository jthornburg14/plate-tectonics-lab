The discovery of plate tectonics

An interactive lab exercise — Physical Geology

Setup

Before you begin

In this lab you will act as a scientific specialist, analyzing real global datasets to discover the patterns that led scientists to develop the theory of plate tectonics.

Read each task carefully before entering your observations. Focus on what you see — not why you think it is that way. Scientific observation comes before interpretation.
Part 1Task 1 & 2

Analyze the maps

Use the map viewer below to study all three scientific datasets. Then focus on your chosen specialty to complete Table 1. You can switch between maps at any time.

Seismology: Earthquake locations 1990–1996 (magnitude 4+). Color = depth: red 0–33 km, orange 33–70 km, green 70–300 km, blue 300–700 km
Volcanology: Red dots = currently or historically active volcanic features (Smithsonian Institution)
Geography: Elevation in meters above sea level. Pink/purple = deepest ocean; cyan = shallow water; green/brown = land; white = highest peaks
Plate boundaries: Bold lines show known plate boundary locations. Use this map with your PDF copy for classification tasks.
Focus on the boundaries — what is the data doing near those bold lines? Is it dense or sparse? Symmetric or asymmetric? Consistent or variable along the boundary?

Forming a hypothesis
